\begin{abstract}
We present three implementations of the AES MixColumns linear transformation
as circuits of 2-input XOR gates over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ that improve the
published depth--count Pareto frontier at every depth from 3 to 5: a
\textbf{97-gate} circuit at depth \textbf{3} (the known minimum depth),
improving the 99-gate record of Shi, Feng, and Xu (ToSC 2023); a
\textbf{92-gate} circuit at depth \textbf{4}, improving the 97-gate point of
Osvik and Canright (ePrint 2024/1076); and an \textbf{89-gate} circuit at
depth \textbf{5}, improving Osvik and Canright's 94-gate depth-5 point by
five gates and shallower than any published circuit of fewer than 94 gates
whose depth is stated. At unconstrained depth the published count floor is
89 (Sun, Yang, and Li, ePrint 2025/1493, depth not stated) and, since July
2026, 88 at depth 7 (Jean, ePrint 2026/1481); Jean's point does not dominate
the circuits here, which remain on the frontier. The circuits were found by a stochastic local search over a
value-set formulation of the shortest-linear-program problem, built around
moves that are individually neutral or worse but globally productive; the
method is fully specified, and both it and the circuits are published as
machine-checkable artifacts with dependency-free Python verifiers and
reproducers. We make no optimality claim on gate counts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and specification}

A circuit is an ordered list of 2-input XOR gates. Signals $s_0,\dots,s_{31}$
are the input bits; gate $k$ produces signal $s_{32+k} = s_a \oplus s_b$ with
$a,b$ both strictly smaller than $32+k$. Cost is the number of gates; depth
counts gates on the longest input-to-output path, with inputs at depth 0.

The bit/byte convention is: the AES state column is four bytes
$a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3$; bit index $i \in \{0,\dots,31\}$ denotes bit $(i \bmod 8)$
of byte $\lfloor i/8 \rfloor$, least-significant-bit first within each byte.
MixColumns maps the input column to the output column with
\[
\mathit{out}[c] \;=\; 2 \cdot a_c \,\oplus\, 3 \cdot a_{(c+1) \bmod 4}
\,\oplus\, a_{(c+2) \bmod 4} \,\oplus\, a_{(c+3) \bmod 4},
\]
with multiplication in $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ modulo $x^8+x^4+x^3+x+1$
(\texttt{0x11b}), following NIST FIPS 197-upd1, Section 5.1.3,
Eq.~5.6~\cite{FIPS197}. Because MixColumns is linear over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$,
agreement on the 32 unit-input vectors is a complete correctness check. Gate
counts and depths are invariant under input/output bit relabeling, so the
comparisons below do not depend on convention choices.

The s-XOR (in-place) cost model is comparable to this one: an s-XOR program is
a sequence of instructions $x_i \leftarrow x_i \oplus x_j$, and unrolling the
overwritten registers (SSA-style) turns a $k$-instruction program into exactly
$k$ free-standing 2-input XOR gates. Published s-XOR counts therefore bound
the published state of the art in our model. Results in other cost models ---
multi-input XOR gates, technology-mapped gate-equivalent area, in-place
quantum CNOT circuits --- are not comparable and are not claimed against.

Depth 3 is the known minimum depth for AES MixColumns (stated e.g.\
in~\cite{SFX23}: an output depending on $w$ inputs needs depth at least
$\lceil \log_2 w \rceil$, and MixColumns has outputs of weight 7), so the
97-gate circuit's contribution is the count at that depth. The 89-gate
depth-5 circuit is shallower than any published circuit of fewer than 94
gates whose depth is stated; the published sub-89 point --- Jean's
88~\cite{Jean26} --- sits at depth 7, so neither dominates the other and
both are on the frontier (the 89 of~\cite{SYL25} states no depth).

\section{Method}

The circuits were found by stochastic local search over a \emph{value-set}
formulation of the shortest-linear-program problem: a circuit is represented
as a set of $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ masks over the 32 inputs, valid iff every mask is
the XOR of two already-available masks (realizability) and all 32 output
masks are present; the gate count is the set size.

The starting observation is that in the published record circuits every gate
is locally efficient --- the signature of greedy construction --- which
suggests such circuits sit at local optima, and that smaller circuits are
separated from them by locally \emph{suboptimal} intermediate steps. The
search is therefore built around moves that are individually neutral or worse
but globally productive:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{remove-1}: drop a non-output mask if the set remains realizable;
\item \textbf{neutral swap (plateau walk)}: remove one mask and add one repair
  mask that restores realizability --- size unchanged --- walking the plateau
  of equal-size circuits until some mask becomes jointly redundant;
\item \textbf{remove-2-add-1 (hub move)}: the compound move that nets $-1$
  when single removals are exhausted;
\item \textbf{destroy-and-rebuild}: remove $k$ masks and greedily
  re-synthesize from a pool of candidate masks, accepting rebuilds that are
  no larger, or slightly larger with fixed probability (the uphill steps);
\item \textbf{depth-aware reconstruction}: a fixpoint computation of each
  mask's minimum build depth over the set, used to reject depth-cap
  violations and to emit gate lists in depth order.
\end{itemize}

The core loop, for the seedable engines, is:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
S := value-set of a starting circuit (or of the naive per-output XOR trees)\\
best := S\\
repeat:\\
\hspace*{1.5em}with probability p\_hub: remove two non-output masks from S\\
\hspace*{1.5em}otherwise: \hspace*{4.05em}remove one non-output mask from S\\
\hspace*{1.5em}if S is no longer realizable:\\
\hspace*{3em}search (bounded, randomized) for one repair mask w with\\
\hspace*{3em}\hspace*{1.5em}S $\cup$ \{w\} realizable; if none, undo and continue\\
\hspace*{1.5em}S := trim(S) \hspace*{1em}// drop masks the 32 outputs no longer need\\
\hspace*{1.5em}if depth-capped and min-build-depth(S) > cap: undo and continue\\
\hspace*{1.5em}if |S| < |best|, or |S| = |best| at strictly lower depth:\\
\hspace*{3em}verify against the GF($2^8$) specification; if correct, best := S
\end{quote}

\noindent With the published reproducer this loop, in dependency-free
single-core Python, cuts the published 92-gate circuit of~\cite{XZLBZ20} to 91
gates in seconds and a 90-gate seed to 89 in about two minutes; the
from-scratch depth-3 constructor below reaches 97 gates in one to three
minutes.

The depth-3 circuit comes from a from-scratch constructor in the same spirit:
each output is modeled as $A \oplus B$ where $A,B$ partition its bits into
depth-$\le 2$ parts shared across outputs and refcount-costed, optimized by
simulated annealing with greedy descent and iterated local search; depth
$\le 3$ holds by construction. Deeper records come from a ladder of parallel
workers: the depth-3 circuit seeds a depth-4 search, whose best seeds depth
5, and so on, while a coordinator continuously re-offers every worker the
best circuit feasible at its depth cap. Workers accept candidates that have
fewer gates \emph{or equal gates at strictly lower depth} (a Pareto
tie-break; the 89-gate circuit moved from depth 6 to depth 5 through exactly
this rule). Every candidate is verified against MixColumns rebuilt from
$\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ before it is accepted (verify-before-claim).

The records are replicable with the published configurations: the from-scratch
97 at depth 3 in about one to three minutes on a single core; the 89 at depth
5 in about ten to fifteen minutes from the shipped 89-at-depth-6 seed; and the
full from-scratch ladder in hours.

Implementation and execution of the search campaigns used LLM coding agents
as programming tools, directed by the author; the moves and acceptance rules
above were designed by the author. The method as published is fully specified
and reproducible with the accompanying dependency-free Python code, with no
AI system in the loop: the from-scratch 97-gate depth-3 circuit reproduces in
minutes on a single core, and the method repository documents the full
lineage of every record, from scratch to 89 gates, with the exact code, logs,
and every intermediate verified circuit of the runs that produced them.

\section{Verification and artifacts}
\label{sec:verification}

The circuits are static, machine-checkable artifacts, published with
verifiers at
\begin{center}
\url{https://github.com/Joe-b-20/aes-mixcolumns-xor-circuits}
\end{center}
and archived permanently at DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21299092}{10.5281/zenodo.21299092}; the
method, pipeline, and run evidence are published at
\begin{center}
\url{https://github.com/Joe-b-20/slp-plateau-search}.
\end{center}
Every claim in this note is decidable from the note alone, with nothing
external trusted: the appendices list every gate of every circuit, Section~1
defines the target map, and because MixColumns is linear, replaying a listing
on the 32 unit-input vectors and comparing against the matrix derived from
Eq.~5.6 of~\cite{FIPS197} is a complete correctness check --- roughly thirty
lines of code in any language. Gate counts and depths are read directly off
the listings.

The published artifacts automate this. Each circuit is verified by two
separately implemented, dependency-free Python paths ---
\texttt{verify.py}, which rebuilds the $32 \times 32$ $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ target
map from the $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ definition (the verifier \emph{is} the
specification), and a clean-room verifier with deterministic random trials
and adversarial rejection tests --- plus Verilog testbenches under Icarus
Verilog, all run by continuous integration. Each appendix states its
circuit's canonical SHA-256, computed over the UTF-8 bytes of the compact
JSON string \texttt{\{"inputCount":32,"gates":[...]\}} and pinned in the
repository's integrity metadata.

\section{Scope and claims}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Not optimality.} Minimum 2-input-XOR circuit size (the Shortest
  Linear Program problem) is NP-hard; we do not prove any of these counts
  minimal. We claim only that they are the smallest we have found or seen
  published as of July 2026.
\item \textbf{Comparisons.} All counts are for the exact model and convention
  of Section~1; the
  baselines~\cite{SFX23,OC24,Max19,XZLBZ20,LXZZ21,YWSZZ24,SYL25,Jean26}
  were source-checked, and a source-by-source audit (with the non-comparable
  cost models explicitly listed, and a dated Corrections section) is
  maintained in the artifact repository's \texttt{PRIOR\_ART.md}. We claim
  the smallest counts at depths 3, 4, and 5, not at unconstrained depth.
\item \textbf{Earlier results.} This note supersedes the project's earlier
  circuits (89 gates at depth 10; 98 at depth 3; 91 at depth 6), which remain
  in the artifact repository for the archival record.
\end{itemize}
