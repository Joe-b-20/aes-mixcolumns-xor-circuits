\begin{abstract}
We give four verified implementations of AES MixColumns as circuits of 2-input
XOR gates over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$. Three improve the published depth--count Pareto
frontier at their depth: \textbf{97} gates at depth \textbf{3}, the minimum
depth, against 99~\cite{SFX23}; \textbf{92} at depth \textbf{4} against
97~\cite{OC24}; \textbf{89} at depth \textbf{5} against 94~\cite{OC24}. The
fourth, \textbf{88} at depth \textbf{7}, \emph{ties} the published gate-count
floor of Jean~\cite{Jean26} --- who has priority --- with an independent
circuit sharing 61 of 88 internal masks. All were found by stochastic local
search over a value-set formulation of the shortest-linear-program problem. No
87 was found: 47 canonical 88-gate circuits have exhaustively empty
remove-$\le 3$ neighbourhoods. Nothing here is claimed optimal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and specification}

A circuit is an ordered list of 2-input XOR gates: $s_0,\dots,s_{31}$ are the
input bits, and gate $k$ produces $s_{32+k} = s_a \oplus s_b$ with $a,b < 32+k$.
Cost is the gate count; depth counts gates on the longest input-to-output path,
inputs at depth 0. Bit index $i$ denotes bit $(i \bmod 8)$ of state byte
$\lfloor i/8 \rfloor$, least-significant-bit first, and MixColumns maps an input
column $a_0,\dots,a_3$ to
\[
\mathit{out}[c] \;=\; 2 \cdot a_c \,\oplus\, 3 \cdot a_{(c+1) \bmod 4}
\,\oplus\, a_{(c+2) \bmod 4} \,\oplus\, a_{(c+3) \bmod 4},
\]
in $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ modulo $x^8+x^4+x^3+x+1$ (\texttt{0x11b}), per NIST FIPS
197-upd1, Eq.~5.6~\cite{FIPS197}. The map is $\mathrm{GF}(2)$-linear, so
agreement on the 32 unit-input vectors is a complete correctness check; counts
and depths are relabeling-invariant, so no comparison below depends on
convention.

The s-XOR (in-place) model is comparable: SSA-unrolling a $k$-instruction s-XOR
program yields exactly $k$ free-standing gates. Multi-input XOR gates,
gate-equivalent area and quantum CNOT counts are different cost functions: not
comparable, not claimed against.

Depth 3 is the known minimum depth --- an output depending on $w$ inputs needs
depth $\ge \lceil \log_2 w \rceil$, and MixColumns has outputs of weight
7~\cite{SFX23} --- so the depth-3 circuit's contribution is its count. The 89 at
depth 5 is shallower than any published circuit of fewer than 94 gates whose
depth is stated, and stays on the frontier: the published sub-89 point, Jean's
88~\cite{Jean26}, measures depth 7 on our transcription --- that note states no
depth, exactly as~\cite{SYL25} does not.

The 88 at depth 7 \emph{ties} Jean's point rather than improving it: Jean's note
was posted 2026-07-23 and has priority. Ours was found 2026-07-26 on a fully
logged own lineage --- from-scratch 97 at depth 3 (235\,s), a 21\,h ladder to 89
at depth 6 (11.0\,h), one reroute to 89 at depth 5 (592\,s),
$\rho^2$-symmetrization to a 94 at depth 5, then 32.9\,min of plateau walking
--- with no imported circuit in the chain, and is a different circuit: 61 of 88
masks shared, Jaccard 0.530. The fifth circuit, 88 at depth 8
(Appendix~\ref{app:mixcolumns_88gates_depth8}), is derived work and dominated:
its seed chain passes through Jean's, $\rho^2$-symmetrized and peeled to 95,
orbit-walked to 92, then unioned with a 91 of our own lineage before the
descent.

\section{Method}

A circuit is represented as a \emph{value set}: masks over the 32 inputs, valid
iff every mask is the XOR of two already-available masks and all 32 output
masks are present; gate count is the set size. Deletion becomes a set
operation, circuits compare by mask overlap regardless of gate order, and a
neighbourhood --- remove $k$ masks, add at most $k-1$ --- becomes finite and
enumerable (Section~\ref{sec:certificates}).

Good circuits at this size \emph{look} locally efficient --- every gate reads as
an optimal step --- and that is what makes them hard to improve: they sit at
local optima, so smaller circuits are separated from them by locally
\emph{suboptimal} steps. (This was true of the naive construction we started
from and of every circuit we produced up to the 89; we make no claim about how
anyone else's circuit was built.) \textbf{Remove-1} drops a non-output mask if the set
stays realizable. The
\textbf{neutral swap} removes one mask and adds one repair mask at unchanged
size: with $A = \mathrm{closure}(S')$ and $\mathit{stuck} = (S' \cup T)
\setminus A$, every single-mask repair lies in $C = \{u \oplus a : u \in
\mathit{stuck},\, a \in A\} \cap P_2(A)$ ($P_2$ = pair sums), so enumerating
$C$ \emph{decides} the move --- measured $|C| \approx 7$, cheaper than the
earlier engine's 24--40 random candidates, which missed 81--82\,\% of existing
repairs. \textbf{Remove-2-add-1} remains a diversifier, though at the 88/89
frontier it is provably empty (Section~\ref{sec:certificates}).
\textbf{Destroy-and-rebuild} removes a group and re-synthesizes from a pool,
accepting rebuilds no larger, or larger on an annealing schedule with reheat;
destroying a \emph{connected cone} of the DAG leaves a hole worth re-planning,
where $k$ unrelated masks are usually just re-derived, and near-miss rebuilds
are peeled before judgement. A fixpoint over minimum build depths rejects cap
violations and emits gate lists in depth order.

Workers run one engine at one depth cap in fixed-length chunks, alternating
walk and rebuild, and a coordinator re-offers each the best circuit feasible at
its cap (the reseeding ladder). A worker accepts fewer gates \emph{or equal
gates at strictly lower depth} --- a Pareto tie-break, and not a cosmetic one:
89 at depth 6 became 89 at depth 5 through it, and both 88s, first seen at
depths 11 and 10, reached depths 7 and 8 seconds later. Every candidate is
verified against MixColumns rebuilt from $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ before acceptance.
The depth-3 circuit comes from a separate constructor: each output is modelled
as $A \oplus B$, $A$ and $B$ partitioning its bits into shared,
refcount-costed depth-$\le 2$ parts, annealed by iterated local search, so
depth $\le 3$ holds by construction.

\paragraph{The v2 engine.} Version 1's circuits came from an engine whose
kernel was then profiled and rewritten~\cite{Methods}: level-BFS depth
relaxation (17.5$\times$ on destroy-and-rebuild, bit-identical trajectories); a
worklist closure with incremental removal queries, and removal of a bug that
re-verified unchanged circuits every plateau iteration --- 78\,\% of walk
runtime (21.9$\times$ on the walk); victim repooling ($\sim$34$\times$ accepted
moves/s); cone destroys ($\sim$12$\times$); exact repair ($\sim$7$\times$
plateau mobility). The walk now runs at 480--640 iterations/s against 70--110.
What found both 88s was not a cleverer move but a cheap plateau walked at
volume: every distinct equal-best mask set is harvested.

\section{Exact local certificates, and structure}
\label{sec:certificates}

To say what is \emph{not} there we decide the neighbourhood question: given a
verified 88-gate circuit and a window of $k$ removed masks, can at most $k-1$
new masks --- each buildable when added, with cascade unlocks between ---
restore all 32 targets? A ``yes'' is an 87-gate circuit; a completed ``no'' is
a proof for that window. Completeness follows from a first-unlock case
analysis: the first unlock must have the newest added mask as a parent, which
pins each added mask to a finite, enumerable shape. Runs above the proved
budget are recorded as
undecided, never as irreducible. The procedure agrees with an independent brute
force 25/25 and 12/12 at $k \le 3$, and on 1\,257 instances including 122
genuine negatives at $k = 4$.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{47 canonical 88-gate circuits} --- Jean's 88, its 12 plateau
  siblings, and a third family's anchor with 33 representatives --- \textbf{have
  exhaustively empty $k \le 3$ shells}: all 1\,540 windows at $k=2$ and all
  27\,720 at $k=3$ each, none reducible. Hence \emph{any 87-gate circuit differs
  from each of the 47 by at least 4 masks.} Stated plainly: all 47 lie in
  Jean's-lineage families --- families 1--2 are Jean's circuit and its siblings,
  and the third family's anchor is itself seeded through Jean's circuit --- so
  this is a rigidity statement about \emph{that} neighbourhood, and the one
  circuit here independent of that lineage, our own 88 at depth 7, is exactly
  the least-certified: it is \emph{not} among the 47, its exhaustive $k \le 3$
  sweep was never run, and it has only 9 exact $k=4$ windows and 8 SAT cone
  windows. The best-certified shell and the independent shell are disjoint.
\item \textbf{Population.} Harvesting mapped $\approx 139\,878$
  distinct 88-gate mask sets, of which 105\,801 are proven irreducible at
  $k=2$. No 88-gate mask set of minimum build depth $\le 6$ has been seen in
  this harvesting — an observation, not a certified claim: no per-circuit
  depth census of the harvest is published. In total $\approx 165$ million
  exact window decisions, zero timeouts, zero reducible windows; all five
  89-gate seeds likewise have empty $k=2$ shells.
\item \textbf{Windowed SAT is evidence, not proof.} A sound windowed CNF
  encoding, solved by Kissat under two soundness-proved symmetry-breaking
  layers, returned UNSAT up to $k=16$ on Jean's 88 and $k=15$ on the
  third-family anchor (whose whole $k \le 12$ regime is UNSAT, 28/28), with
  \textbf{0 SAT anywhere} --- but UNSAT there is relative to the encoding's
  fixed slot order, and undecided windows are undecided.
\end{itemize}

None of this bounds 87 away globally; it is consistent with 88 being locally
rigid, and it redirected the search: at 88 gates every move class small enough
to enumerate is empty, so what remains is walking the equal-size plateau
widely.

\paragraph{Symmetry.} Byte rotation $\rho$ (order 4) commutes with MixColumns,
so the 32 targets form 8 $\rho$-orbits of size 4 and equivariant circuits can be
searched in orbit space --- but full $\rho$-symmetry costs about $+19$ gates.
The half-rotation $\rho^2$ is where the structure is: in the elite basin
79--81\,\% of a circuit's masks have their $\rho^2$-image also in the mask set,
and the 12 $\rho^2$-fixed masks the search settles on are exactly the classic
$x_0 \oplus x_2$ / $x_1 \oplus x_3$ pairs, rediscovered rather than imposed.
Jean's 88, at 75\,\% (66 of 88), symmetrizes into a second, distinct basin. A
$\rho^2$-equivariant orbit engine produced two \emph{exactly}
$\rho^2$-symmetric 90-gate circuits (depths 9 and 7), improving the best
exactly symmetric circuit we know of from 94 to 90; both are certified locally
optimal in orbit space, and both are derived work, each seed union containing a
circuit symmetrized from Jean's 88. Symmetry is also a seeding device: the
exactly $\rho^2$-symmetric 94 at depth 5 --- 82 of its 94 masks shared with the
89 at depth 5 --- costs $+5$ over that 89 and is the seed from which the 88 at
depth 7 was found.

\section{Verification, artifacts, and reproduction}
\label{sec:verification}

Every circuit here is checkable from the note alone: the appendices list every
gate, Section~1 defines the target map, and because MixColumns is linear,
replaying a listing on the 32 unit-input vectors against
Eq.~5.6~\cite{FIPS197} is a complete correctness check. Each appendix also
states its circuit's canonical SHA-256 over the UTF-8 bytes of
\texttt{\{"inputCount":32,"gates":[...]\}}.

The artifacts automate that. Circuits and verifiers are at
\url{https://github.com/Joe-b-20/aes-mixcolumns-xor-circuits}, archived at
concept DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21299092}{10.5281/zenodo.21299092};
\texttt{python3 verify\_all.py} runs \texttt{verify.py}, which rebuilds the
target map from the $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ definition so the verifier \emph{is} the
specification, a clean-room verifier with adversarial rejection tests, and
Verilog testbenches, all under continuous integration. The method, pipeline,
untouched run archives with each run's exact code, certificate summaries and
the credited transcriptions of the imported circuits~\cite{Jean26,SYL25} are
published separately~\cite{Methods}, one reproduction command per result.
Measured there on 2026-07-27 with the shipped v2 engine, and reported as
measurements rather than promises: the from-scratch 97 at depth 3 in 81\,s
on one core; the 89 at depth 5 in 19\,s and 22\,s from shipped seeds, against
592\,s for the archived 2026-07-14 run on the v1 engine; the 88 at depth 7 in
19.4 and 31.0 minutes in two single-worker re-runs from the $\rho^2$-symmetric
94, against 32.9\,min in the archived ten-worker run; the 92 at depth 4 a
from-scratch cascade of hours. The re-runs are re-runs, not independent
confirmations.

Implementation and execution used LLM coding agents as programming tools,
directed by the author, who designed the moves and acceptance rules. Concretely:
the three frontier records of this note are reproduced by the published,
dependency-free code with no AI system in the loop, whereas the v2 engine, the
certificates of Section~\ref{sec:certificates} and both 88-gate circuits came
out of a 24-agent LLM campaign over 2026-07-26/27, again author-directed. That
distinction changes nothing about correctness: every circuit any of it produced
is machine-verified against MixColumns rebuilt from $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$, and the
method is fully specified and reproducible from the accompanying code either
way.

\section{Scope and claims}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Not optimality.} Minimum 2-input-XOR circuit size (the Shortest
  Linear Program problem) is NP-hard; no count here is proven minimal, and the
  certificates of Section~\ref{sec:certificates} are local.
\item \textbf{Comparisons.} All counts are for the model and convention of
  Section~1; the
  baselines~\cite{SFX23,OC24,Max19,XZLBZ20,LXZZ21,YWSZZ24,SYL25,Jean26} were
  source-checked, and a source-by-source audit, with a dated Corrections
  section, is in the artifact repository's \texttt{PRIOR\_ART.md}. We claim the
  smallest counts at depths 3, 4 and 5, and no improvement at unconstrained
  depth: the published floor is 88 and stays 88.
\item \textbf{Priority.} The 88 at depth 7 ties~\cite{Jean26}, posted first; the
  88 at depth 8 is derived from that circuit and dominated. Neither is a record
  claim. The depths quoted for~\cite{Jean26} and~\cite{SYL25} --- 7 and 9 ---
  are our measurements of our own transcriptions; neither paper states a depth.
\item \textbf{Earlier results.} This note supersedes the project's 98 at depth
  3, 91 at depth 6 and 89 at depth 10, kept in the artifact repository.
\end{itemize}
